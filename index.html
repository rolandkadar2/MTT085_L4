Stresses in Pa, η in Pa·s, γ in s⁻¹. The elastic contribution is zero for a Newtonian fluid.Elastic contribution from N1 = 2Jeσ12² with Je = 10⁻⁴ Pa⁻¹, and N2 = −N1/10.

Flow

  • Two infinite parallel plates, gap h. Only v1(t, x2) is non-zero.
  • Incompressible, isothermal, laminar, no slip, no gravity. ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
ρ ∂v1∂t = Δpl + ∂σ12∂x2,   Δp = p1 − p2,   l = 10 mm v1(0) = vlower,   v1(h) = vupper

Simple shear (first page): Δp = 0 and vlower = 0.

Fluid

σ12 = η(γ) γ,   γ = ∂v1∂x2 Newtonian: η = constant Carreau–Yasuda: η = η0 [1 + (λγ)a](n−1)/a,   η∞ = 0
  • Generalised Newtonian fluid: elasticity does not enter the flow calculation.
  • Numerical floor η ≥ 10⁻⁷ Pa·s, because with η∞ = 0 and n near 0 the shear rate is unbounded.

Normal stresses

N1 = σ11 − σ22 = 2Jeσ122,   Je = 10⁻⁴ Pa⁻¹ N2 = σ22 − σ33 = −N1/10 σe11 = N1 + N2,   σe22 = N2,   σe33 = 0
  • Computed afterwards from the local shear stress; non-Newtonian fluid only.
  • The quadratic law is the low-shear-rate limit and overestimates N1 at high stress. Je is an order of magnitude for a polymer melt.

Steady state, Newtonian fluid

v1 = Δp2ηl x2(h − x2) + vlower + (vupper − vlower) x2h σ12 = Δpl (h2 − x2) + η vupper − vlowerh

Transient

  • Inertia only: momentum diffuses from the plates with diffusivity η/ρ. The stress growth of a real polymer melt, set by its relaxation time, is not modelled.
  • Time to steady state, Newtonian start from rest (stress at the fixed plate within 1 %):
ts ≈ 0.45 ρh2η
  • Non-Newtonian: η is replaced by the tangent viscosity dσ12/dγ, averaged (geometric mean) over the gap. This sets the playback speed only.
  • “Steady state” on the first page: shear rate at the fixed plate within 1 % of v/h. In the example flows: estimated remaining velocity change below 1 % of the largest velocity.
  • Changing the gap: each layer keeps its velocity at its height; squeeze flow is ignored.
  • Playback is slow motion, 1.5 to 5 s on screen; longer transients are sped up after 5 s.

Numerics and drawing

  • Finite differences on 64 intervals across the gap, implicit Euler in time, viscosity taken from the previous time step.
  • First page: positive σ12 is drawn to the left. Example flows: the magnitude |σ12| is drawn; the component σ12 = η ∂v1/∂x2 itself changes sign where the velocity gradient does. The plate readouts give the force per area of the fluid on each plate, positive in x1.
  • First page: arrow lengths follow the sliders (linear velocity for the Newtonian fluid, logarithmic otherwise; logarithmic stress).
  • Example flows: arrow lengths are relative to the largest steady velocity and stress. The pressure sliders end at the Δp that gives a wall shear rate of 6000 s⁻¹ in a 1 mm gap.
